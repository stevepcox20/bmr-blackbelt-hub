Digital Leverage — Content Is the Highest-Leverage Asset You Have

Maria Wendt went from $63 in her first year of coaching to a million-dollar-a-month, low-ticket digital business — working two days a week, writing every hook herself, with a content team of three or four. Her core idea: most business owners are chasing clients, traffic and talent directly, in an exhausting outward motion. Content creation collapses all three into one input. The whole system runs on simplicity, not sophistication.

01
The Leverage Problem
Why media was the only leverage available to her

Maria learned the concept from Naval Ravikant's The Almanack of Naval Ravikant. Naval describes four kinds of leverage a business can pull: labor (a team working for you), capital (money to deploy), code (build software once, sell it forever), and media (create once, distribute for free, at scale).

Starting out, she had none of the first three — no team, no capital, no coding skill. Media was the only lever left, and it was also the newest and cheapest one in history: 50 years ago a mass-market ad cost hundreds of thousands of dollars and still couldn't be tracked. Now it's free, in her pocket, and fully measurable.

What media leverage actually unlocks

Without it

Feast-or-famine lead flow — drill for leads, work the leads, close, then dry again. Constant outward chasing: more clients, more traffic, more talent, all as separate battles.

With it

A daily, steady, "drinking from a fire hose" lead flow — the problem becomes handling too many leads, not finding them. Higher credibility. Better hires (people already know and trust the brand before they apply).

"Content creation accelerates the process of people trusting you. We do so much on the back end — VSLs, webinars, email sequences, sales scripts — to get people to trust us. With content, they come to you piping hot, ready to buy. You're not chasing them down."

02
The Identity Shift
Not a coach, not a business owner — a content creator

The shift she asks the room to make: "For the purposes of this conversation, you are not a coach, you're not a business owner — you are a content creator. That's your job." The identity shift matters because it changes what you protect first when the business is on fire.

Part of the shift is learning to regulate your own emotional reaction to business fires — team drama, client drama, "legal stuff" — and stop treating them as more urgent than the upload schedule. Her framing: if you wait for a day with no fires before you create content, that day never comes.

Her one true non-negotiable

Verbatim

"Content creation is my only non-negotiable. The only thing I will not allow to not happen is me hitting my content schedule. I will hit my upload schedule come hell or high water — and I mean that."

Proof point: after a 26-hour, 3-flight trip to the Thailand intensive, she went straight from the hotel room to steaming 17 dresses and filming ~70 videos before doing anything else. Also: she once woke at 3am to write scripts for a filming day she'd procrastinated on, rather than miss the shoot.

Why this is "the house burning on fire issue"

Her stated single takeaway for the room: if you are not creating content on a consistent basis, that is the most urgent problem in your business right now — ahead of anything else competing for your attention.

03
The Three Rules
What she actually does, mechanically
1

One platform at a time, to 100k, then move

Spreading across five platforms at once means looking "pretty shitty on five" instead of great on one. Go all-in on every feature of one platform until it hits 100k followers/members, then start the next. Pick the platform you personally consume the most — not "where your clients are." She built Facebook group → email → Instagram → YouTube, one at a time, in that order.

2

Zero editing

Her first ~125,000 YouTube subscribers came from sitting at an iMac, pressing record, pressing stop, uploading — no editing. Her read: unpolished, unprofessional-looking content builds trust faster because it removes the "production" layer and puts all the attention on what's actually being said.

3

Only two content types: Tutorials and Taboo

No content calendar, no "Testimonial Tuesday" structure — she finds structure adds friction. Every post is one of two things, broken down below.

Tutorials
Step-by-step, broken down to an almost absurd granular level — like a recipe where skipping any step means you don't get the result. Her example: most people teach "how to check email" as one step. She teaches it as: press the power button → enter your password → find the Safari icon → press it. "Make the tutorials you wish you had" when you were starting out — that specificity is hard to do, which is exactly why it's a blue ocean.
Taboo
The things most people in a niche won't say out loud — exact income down to the penny, exact expenses, getting sued, getting divorced, single motherhood, faith. Calls this her "knock and build" strategy: something that knocks you down a peg in social standing, paired with something that builds you back up, creates real tension people trust. "You think you want authority, but you actually want trust."

Sourcing taboo content: track what you feel an emotion about in daily life — sad, angry, proud, whatever — that's a story worth telling. For sensitive topics (her divorce), she waited about a year before posting, which let her handle it "with a lot more class and dignity." The lesson isn't venting for venting's sake — it's "I went through this, I survived it, here's what I learned." Start with industry-taboo (money, business mistakes) before personal-taboo (divorce, single parenting) — personal is a harder skill.

The Workflow Ladder
Solo → one team member → a small content team

2
+1 Team Member
Idea in Notes app → texted straight to the team member → they pick footage, cut the reel, schedule it → she gives final approval → track and repurpose (~80% of content is repurposed).
3
3–4 Team Members
Same core loop, more hands. Now deliberately working down the awareness pyramid into mass-market content.

The actual test she gives the room: don't copy her specific workflow — audit your own life and ask "what is the most likely way for me to create content in a way I'll actually stick with?" A workflow you build in one inspired burst and don't personally enjoy won't survive contact with a normal week.

05
The One Metric That Matters
Views-to-followers ratio, not raw views

The team's north star metric right now is Instagram followers (target: 1M by year end). To decide whether a piece of content is worth repurposing or boosting with ad spend, they don't look at views — they look at the views-to-followers ratio.

Bad ratio
Good ratio
100,000 views, 2 new followers — "happens sometimes," and it's a signal to leave that piece alone.
10,000 views, 2,000 new followers — immediately flagged to the ad team to run paid spend behind it.
~$500/dayAd spend, only on proven high-ratio content
80%Of content is repurposed, not new
1–2%Of followers actually notice repeat content

On repurposing without anyone noticing: she repeats content to the point of being "physically nauseous" saying the same thing — exact reposts, same script in a different outfit, or a small hook tweak. Her reasoning: only 1–2% of followers see any given post, so repetition reads as fresh to almost everyone, and the handful of lines she's repeated for years are the ones that have actually landed and stuck for her audience (e.g. "I made $63 my first year").

06
What She Still Won't Delegate
The one job that stays with the business owner
Verbatim

"Some things in the business are best done by the business owner. You should be outsourcing domestic work — there's only so many ways to fold a towel. Have someone else fold your towels, fold your socks. But the highest-leverage points of the business — for me, that's writing every word we put out — are still done by me. That's how I make my money."

Even with a 3–4 person content team producing 30–40 posts a day across platforms, she still writes every hook and script herself. The team's job is production and scheduling — not the message.

07
Rapid-Fire Q&A
Selected answers from the floor
Repurposing How do you decide if a piece gets repurposed?
Views-to-followers ratio, not raw views. A 10,000-view post that brings 2,000 followers is a great ratio and gets repurposed hard. A 100,000-view post that brings 2 followers gets left alone, even though the view count looks better on paper.
Targeting Should content differ by awareness level as you scale?
Optimise for the most-aware tip of the pyramid first, always — "if you're speaking to everybody, you're talking to nobody." She only started working down into broader, less-aware audiences after fully dominating her specific niche.
Format mix What's the ratio between B-roll/trending audio and talking-head content?
No fixed ratio — entirely account-dependent. Some of her students blew up on carousels, others on talking heads. Use the views-to-followers metric to find which style works for your specific account, then lean hard into that one.
08
BMR Application
Where this maps onto Steve's content and Alex

Simplicity is the actual lesson, not a caveat. Maria's whole run to $1M/month has zero editing, one content calendar rule ("only two types"), and no fancy tagging system. BMR's weekly content system (one Question, four angles: Connection / Character / Coaching / Flex+CTA) is already this simple. The instinct after a session like this is to add more structure -- resist it. If anything, look at what's been added to the system since it locked and ask whether it's still earning its place.

Tutorials + Taboo maps onto Wednesday and the Story Bank. "Tutorial" content is Wednesday's teaching slot -- the granular, step-by-step how (Chapter 10, the WER Loop, a specific breath practice, broken down to the same absurd specificity Maria uses). "Taboo" is the territory Story Bank and "Hot Topics Not Confession" already occupy -- naming what men don't say out loud about the pattern, without a depth-of-struggle confessional. Maria's rule to start with industry-taboo before personal-taboo lines up with the existing guardrail on not generating Steve's personal recovery content unprompted.

Views-to-followers ratio is a sharper lens for the locked dark-social metrics. BMR already tracks visits/clicks/sign-ups/DMs over comments. Maria's specific mechanic -- a ratio that flags which pieces deserve repurposing or ad spend, rather than which got the most raw views -- is a concrete way to apply that principle once there's enough volume of posts to compare.

The non-negotiable upload schedule and "I still write every word": both directly reinforce two things already locked -- Steve line-approving every script (Question Is The Hook), and the Master Rule copywriter test. Maria's team ships 30-40 posts a day with a content team, but the actual words are still hers. Same principle, smaller scale.
