Scale with Scissors — Growth Comes From Cutting, Not Adding

Laura Higgins on why the "throw spaghetti at the wall" mentality that works when you're starting a coaching business actively breaks past $1M/year — and the practical exercise for deciding what to prioritise, protect, delegate, and delete.

01
The Everest Pack Problem
What works starting out actively breaks past $1M

"Starting out in Black Belt, you have to throw spaghetti at the wall — testing niche, marketing, sales mechanism, delivery. That mentality carried into a million-dollar-a-year business just starts to feel terrible and heavy. Like the people climbing Everest with a massive pack — to get to the next level, you can't keep carrying that much."

The problem isn't just marketing — there are things already inside your offer, your delivery, and your sales process adding noise without adding results. As a business scales, it gets genuinely harder to tell which specific funnel, piece of content, or delivery element is actually driving the outcome.

02
Find the One Real Number
Vanity metrics vs. what you're actually optimising for

Marketing invites you to want everything at once — great ROAS, cheap leads, a million followers, YouTube subscribers. Pick the one number that actually matters (for her: qualified calls booked) and ignore the rest.

Free lead magnet
$9 paid offer
$2 cost per lead — but only 3% of leads converted through to a booked strategy session.
Costs more per lead — but 33% converted through to a booked strategy session. A 10x lift in the metric that actually matters.

Confirmed with a paid-ads specialist reviewing her full funnel stack: one specific funnel was the actual seven-figure driver, with the lowest cost-per-booked-call of everything running. Know that number before deciding where to put more money and energy.

03
Ditch the Shoulds
You're the boss — you get to choose

Once you know your one real number, you can stop chasing "shiny object" strategies that are crushing it for someone else but don't feel aligned for you. Only 2-3 things per vertical (marketing, sales, delivery) should get real focus — and they should be things you can execute without watching the clock or overthinking.

04
Burnout & Active Recovery
"Burnout happens when we stop having fun" — Taki

Personal account: hosting a client event, her "social battery died" mid-conversation with a client, she had to ask her team for five minutes to go cry in her hotel room before pushing through one final session — then never actually circled back afterward to process it.

Active recovery: athletes have a fully-locked-in game day, then deliberately don't train hard the next day — recovery is scheduled, not left to chance. Founders tend to just keep performing, performing, performing without ever scheduling the recovery half of that cycle, and lose themselves in the process.

Her message to the room: if you're in that season right now, it will get better — the scariest part of burnout is believing you'll never get your spark back, and that belief is false.

05
Depth, Not Breadth
Greatest hits on rotation beat a new offer every month

Building a new workshop every month meant she was creating curriculum, content, YouTube, emails and a fresh funnel simultaneously — and workshops on off-topic ideas tanked. The fix: identify her actual "greatest hits" — the 2-3 topics that reliably generate leads — and keep them in permanent rotation instead of inventing something new each cycle.

Why repetition converts better: having run the same workshop 10+ times means she knows her best stories, hits her cues, and converts better than the first time she ever ran it. Reference cited in the room: John Demartini has delivered his signature talk 1,848 times.

"Choose your hard": same content to a new audience, or new content to the same audience — trying to do both (new content and new audience, every cycle) is what actually burns people out. One attendee's counterpoint: greatest hits stopped working for him because he wasn't growing his list — repetition only works if there's a large enough or continually refreshed audience seeing it.

06
The Money / Energy Matrix
Her own spin on the Eisenhower matrix — live exercise

Prioritise

Makes money + gives energy. Should only be a handful of things (for her: creating content, delivering to clients).

Protect

Gives energy, doesn't make money — can be business or personal (Pilates, sauna, walking the dog). This is how you burnout-proof the business.

Delegate

Makes money, drains energy, but is essential to keep the business running. Hand to a person or to AI — doesn't matter which.

Delete

Doesn't make money, drains energy, and isn't essential. Every attendee was asked to actually delete one real thing before leaving the room.

Rule for the exercise: keep it strictly business-related. And the "essential" test for the delegate box is genuinely: does removing this stop the business from operating? If not, it's a delete candidate, not a delegate candidate.

07
Q&A: Fixing an Underperforming Funnel
Selected answers from the floor

Diagnosing a funnel that's technically running but underperforming: the issue often isn't the whole strategy — it's one specific piece, often the opt-in offer itself. Her suggestion: consider whether the current lead magnet is the right top-of-funnel piece at all, or whether it should be relocated further down the funnel (the same move she made with her own VSL — shifted from top-of-funnel to "watch this before your call").

A squeeze-page qualification tactic, shared by another attendee

Build a hyper-specific opt-in page naming exactly who it's for — accept a low conversion rate (5-10%) in exchange for a Meta pixel trained specifically on that avatar. Add a VSL on the same page, then a 10-minute typeform application before anyone can book a call — disqualifying 60-70% of remaining leads. By the time someone is actually on the phone, they're roughly 90% pre-qualified.

Pricing test result (hers): free masterclass = low lead quality. Adding a $9 price tag: lead quality roughly 10x'd. Also tested $19 against $9 — $9 won consistently.

Live paid workshops have shifted from top-of-funnel to middle-of-funnel: sometimes upsold directly at checkout to lift average order value, rather than used to drive new $9 sign-ups.

BMR Application
Where this maps onto BMR's launch and content load

The lead magnet decision, seen through the $9-vs-free data: BMR's lead magnet is still undecided (arc audit vs stage audit, target was Jul 28-Aug 1). Laura's finding is worth weighing directly -- a small friction point (even a short paid step, or a genuinely specific qualifying question) may filter for men who are actually ready to act, not just curious, and could produce a higher-quality sign-up than a fully frictionless free download.

Greatest hits over new content every cycle: the locked BMR content strategy (organise by audience Question, not monthly themes) already behaves like a greatest-hits rotation -- a strong Question and its four-post treatment can be revisited and re-angled rather than replaced. Worth protecting that discipline explicitly rather than feeling pressure to invent a new framework every month.

The Money/Energy matrix as a personal exercise for Steve: a concrete, reusable filter for what actually earns a place on his plate right now -- module-writing, content, launch admin, Companion Copy backlog -- versus what's just adding load without moving the launch forward. Directly relevant given the current stack of open items (Offer Diamond loose ends, Companion Copy gaps, social account setup, lead magnet).
