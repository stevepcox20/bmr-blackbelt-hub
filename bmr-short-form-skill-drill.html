The Short Form Skill Drill — Turn One Topic Into a Week of Bangers

A structured live drill for going from a single topic to filmed short-form content, on camera, in one sitting. The whole thing rests on one line: what will always outperform any kind of structure is the energy and belief you bring to it.

4Angles per topic (Problem/Proof/Philosophy/Plan)
4Parts to every banger (Hook/Build/Payoff/Invitation)
2xFilmed live, with feedback between takes
01
Energy Beats Structure
Why topic selection matters more than getting the format "right"

"What will always outperform any kind of structure is the energy and belief that you have behind it."

The case study behind it: deliberately picking a higher-level, less "sexy" workshop topic drew half the usual registrations, but produced the sixth-largest event ever run and the strongest application quality. Fewer people showed up excited about the topic on paper. The ones who did got real results, because the teacher was genuinely fired up about it.

The separation that matters: your emotional dashboard (how excited you feel about a topic) is not the same instrument as your business metrics (registrations, views). Optimise for genuine energy first -- the numbers follow, sometimes from a smaller room than expected.

02
Topic Selection
Narrowing from "everything" to one filmable idea
  1. Start with your business model's core arms -- for a coaching business, usually Attract / Convert / Deliver.
  2. Pick ONE arm you're genuinely excited about right now, not the one you think you "should" cover.
  3. Drill down to a single subtopic inside that arm.

Topic selection ranks above structural perfection every time. A flat, dutiful topic filmed perfectly loses to a genuinely alive topic filmed rough.

03
The Four Angles Framework
One topic, four different lenses -- 30 seconds per column

"When you hold up a diamond and spin it, the light catches different facets -- we're just looking at the same topic through different lenses."

Problem

The pains people feel and the mistakes they make.

Proof

Your results, client results, or you as the example.

Philosophy

What the industry/conventional wisdom gets wrong, and your better way.

Plan

The ordered steps or timeline for actually doing it.

04
The Anatomy of Every "Banger"
Four sequential parts to every piece of content
The four parts

Hook (3-5 seconds, the scroll-stop, newspaper-headline energy) → Build (the bulk -- story, list, or ordered steps) → Payoff (the aha moment or punchline) → Invitation (the explicit next action).

Build order is flexible -- some creators hook-first, some payoff-first. What doesn't move is that all four parts show up somewhere.

05
Invitation Strategy
Not every call-to-action carries the same weight
1

Save / Share

The strongest signal. People will lurk-save-share a stranger's content specifically to avoid a sales DM -- it's a low-commitment way to say "this mattered."

2

Follow

Third strongest, but still a real signal of ongoing interest.

3

Comment a keyword

Only worth using when it's actually routing into an automated DM sequence. Otherwise it's a weak ask.

"I don't really care about comments or likes." They're the weakest engagement signal in the stack -- design the CTA around save/share/follow, not vanity metrics.

06
Keyword Design Rules
Only relevant once comment-to-DM automation exists
  • Maximum 7 characters. 8 won't break the automation, but shorter always wins.
  • Easy to spell AND easy to say out loud -- test it, people misspell even simple 5-letter words.
  • Avoid a word your audience already uses constantly in normal conversation -- it'll misfire against everyday DMs.
  • Anchor the keyword to something visible in the video itself.

07
The Hookinator Move
Using AI to stack hook options, fast

Feed a voice-based tool the audience description, the topic, the build, and the payoff. It returns a stack of distinct hook options -- both the on-screen text and the spoken opening line. Practical everyday use: drop an existing email into the tool and ask for a reel-format conversion of the same idea.

08
Live Filming Discipline
Shoot twice, get feedback between takes

"Unhinged you is way better than LinkedIn you."

Technical

  • Vertical phone orientation.
  • Shoot the hook three times at different energy levels -- normal, dialed up, calm.
  • Either shoot the build continuously, or pause between beats for notes.

Delivery

  • Name a real person in your world instead of an abstract audience.
  • Speak like you're talking to your best friend.
  • Avoid forced energy mismatches -- "Captain Jazz Hands" syndrome.
  • Calibrate to your natural self at 10/10, not an artificial performance.

"The most important thing is that the best of you gets to show up." First takes often suck. The seventh take sometimes wins. Good content beats a perfect delivery.

09
Worked BMR Example
Running the Four Angles on a real BMR subtopic

Topic: "Why I couldn't stop even though I wanted to" (Freedom From Sexual Urges arc).

Problem

Alex assumes wanting to stop should be enough. It never is -- willpower alone almost always fails, and he thinks that means he's broken.

Proof

Steve's own trained-habit story (masturbating during exam study, the false "I'm not good at this" trigger) -- self-as-example, not a client case.

Philosophy

Recovery culture sells willpower and shame. BMR's contrarian take: it's a trained neural pathway, not a character flaw, and trained things can be retrained.

Plan

Notice the trigger without suppressing it, name what it's actually standing in for, redirect without white-knuckling.

BMR Application
Where this maps directly onto the BMR content system

The Banger structure is already BMR's structure. Hook/Build/Payoff/Invitation is the same shape as the existing Hook Framework and the question-first video script format -- this session gives a faster live drill for generating it, not a new format to learn.

The Four Angles is a genuinely new tool. BMR doesn't currently have a fast way to spin one topic into four distinct posts. Mapped to the week structure: Problem fits Monday/Connection, Proof fits Tuesday/Character (Steve's own story), Philosophy fits Wednesday/Coaching, Plan fits the Thursday/Flex CTA post.

Steve already has a tool called the Hookinator (the copy-review loop used on Module content) -- this session's "Hookinator move" is the same idea applied live, on camera, before filming rather than after drafting. Worth testing as a pre-filming step, not just a post-draft review.

Invitation ranking (save/share over comments) validates the current distribution approach -- BMR already tracks dark-social metrics (visits/clicks/sign-ups/DMs, not comments) per the locked distribution rule. This session gives the reasoning why that's the right instinct.

Filming discipline transfers directly to the iPhone + decent light + clean audio production standard already locked for BMR video. "Unhinged you is way better than LinkedIn you" is the same instruction as filming substance over studio polish.
