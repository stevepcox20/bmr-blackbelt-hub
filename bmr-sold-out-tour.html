The Sold Out Tour — Make a Record So Good the Tour Sells Itself

Taki's own flagship framework from the Thailand intensive: content as singles, a monthly body of work as an album, and the live paid offer as the tour. The core reversal — stop gatekeeping the good stuff. There are no secrets left in the AI era; what people actually buy now is presence.

01
The Science of Yay
Reverse-engineering your own growth timeline

Taki plotted his own business timeline and asked, for each real jump in growth, "what happened right before this?" Four growth phases, each triggered by something specific -- a book that led to an event that led to a matching program; a pivot forced by circumstance where being first to speak honestly won trust; guest-to-client events; and most recently, monthly paid workshops plus YouTube.

"Each growth phase started when three things were true: I had some maker time to figure out what was true and real. Because of that, the content I made was honest and useful for the moment. And I wasn't selling a program — I was inviting people to something I was personally convicted was awesome, right now."

The flat periods were the exact opposite: always-on, no time to create, content going vanilla because there was nothing exciting happening to draw from, and pitching started to feel like selling a product instead of an invitation.

The exercise he ran live: sketch your own timeline, mark the real growth spurts, and identify what you started, stopped, or made just before each one.

02
Singles, Albums, Tours
The core framework of the session
Single
A dangerous idea (a contrarian take) with a catchy tune (a hook + build, said as a soundbite). Plays for free on "the radio" — every social channel — and lives rent-free in their head. You know it's landed when prospects start repeating your own phrases back at you.
Album
A complete body of work about one real, true-right-now topic. Priced free or as an impulse buy. Consuming it should make someone want to spend more time and money with you.
Tour
The live, high-ticket experience. Same songs as the album — not new, unheard material — delivered in a way only live can do. This is the actual coaching program/offer.
03
Presence, Not Secrets
Stop gatekeeping — there are no secrets left

"In the old days we sold secrets — we had the answer, they didn't, so come pay me to learn it. There are no secrets anymore. If I'm 59 or younger, I'm asking Claude or ChatGPT, or I'm on YouTube. What people buy now is presence, not secrets."

Why someone still goes to a concert even though they could stream the album: they want to hear the songs they already know, sung live, with the artist in the room. The tour isn't a shrunk-down, censored version of the album — it's the identical material delivered in a way that can only happen live.

"Relevance is like rent. You have to pay it every month, or you get evicted from people's minds and their wallets."

04
Make Time / Market Time / Meet Time
The rhythm that replaces "always on"
M
Make Time
In the studio, laying down songs — "making bangers"
M
Market Time
Promoting the record — "making bank"
M
Meet Time
Fully unplugged, off — "making babies"

Same cycle athletes and touring musicians run — game day, training, off-season. Being always-on is what makes content go vanilla and eventually burns founders out.

05
Daily Demos
See something → Make something → Say something

Not every piece of content needs to be good on the first try — the goal is volume of reps. Cited study: two pottery classes, one told to make the single best pot of the semester, the other told to make as many pots as possible. The quantity group made more and better pots, because more attempts beat one precious attempt.

1

See something

Practical version: once a week (or daily), scroll back through your own camera roll from the last week or two and look for anything that sparked a flicker of interest. Don't try to force new ideas from nothing.

2

Make something

Connect what you noticed to your prospects, your business, or your market.

3

Say something

Ship a piece of content built from that connection — the same day, while it's fresh.

Speed matters: "the longer the gap between I see something and I say something, the shittier it gets." Do it hot, not batched days later.

06
Sticky Singles — The Pocket Banger
Turning a raw daily demo into something polished
Every banger piece of content has four parts

Hook (grabs attention) → Build (the story or list of steps) → Payoff (the punchline) → Invitation (the explicit next action/CTA).

Take your best daily demos, keep the ones with legs, and polish them into this structure rather than starting every piece of content from a blank page.

07
Building a Real Album
One complete body of work, on a monthly cycle

His own production cycle: two weeks a month are dedicated to one complete body of work around a single topic — daily demos, reels, a carousel, videos, all on the same theme, culminating in a paid monthly workshop. The workshop is the album; everything else is raw ingredient and promotion for it.

Picking the next album — 4 quick prompts (10 seconds each, first-thought only)

You

What's hot for you right now — what's working really well in your business at the moment?

Clients

What are your current clients frothing on right now — what are they loving that you're doing with them?

Market

What's everyone talking about, worried about, loving, hating right now?

Trend stance

Given the trend, are you going to ride it or buck it — and what does the market need from a leader like you, right now?

08
The Tour Itself
What actually makes the live version worth paying more for

You Live

Not just teaching it live — being in the room, sharing what's genuinely fresh and new right now.

Them Singing

Customisation — applying your material to their specific situation, live, in front of you.

Us Together

The group energy and peer help that simply can't happen over Zoom.

"Hot Ticket" self-assessment (1-4 scale, two separate questions): (1) how exciting/hot is your current offer, honestly, right now? (2) separately, regardless of how exciting it actually is — how good a job are you doing telling people it's exciting? Most attendees scored their offer's actual quality higher than their communication of that quality — the gap to close is usually promotion, not the product.

09
The Drop — Deciding the Album's Format
Three quick decisions before you start creating

Format

Written, Audio, Video, or Live. Pick whichever matches your actual strength, not what's trendy.

Pricing

Free or cheap. Rule of thumb: high demand → charge for it. Low demand → give it away free to build demand first.

Length ("pick a ticket")

Minutes, hours, days, or weeks — matched to how much time people are realistically willing to spend on it.

BMR Application
Where this maps directly onto the BMR content system

Singles/Albums/Tours is already the shape of BMR's weekly system: the daily posts (Connection/Character/Coaching/Flex+CTA/Easy) are singles, the full week's arc around one audience Question is the album, and The Relationship Rescue itself is the tour -- the live, paid, presence-based delivery of material that's already been given away in the free content. Naming this explicitly makes it easier to judge whether a given week is actually a coherent "album" or just five disconnected singles.

Daily Demos is the exact mechanic behind the locked "the answer can come from anywhere" rule. BMR already says Monday/Tuesday/Friday content can pull from a personal story, something Chez said, a movie, a sporting moment. Taki's practice -- scroll the camera roll from the last week or two for whatever sparked a flicker of interest -- is a concrete, lightweight habit Steve could run weekly to source that content, rather than needing a fresh idea from nothing each time.

Presence, not secrets validates the Companion Copy / video approach: giving away the real mechanism in every post doesn't cannibalise The Relationship Rescue -- what Alex pays for is Steve's direct process and presence, not information he couldn't get free. This is independent confirmation the current instinct (real conversation, not gatekept teasers) is the right one.

Hot Ticket self-score is a useful gut-check for the sales page: given the still-open Offer Diamond items (the [DATE] placeholder, the payment-parity flag) and the sales page not yet live, this is a concrete question worth asking before launch -- is the program itself under-communicated, or is there a genuine gap in the offer? Worth running honestly before assuming the fix is more content rather than clearer communication.
